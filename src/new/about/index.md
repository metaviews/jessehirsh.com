Jesse Hirsh works on the questions institutions get stuck on when technology moves faster than their ability to govern it: what to automate, what to keep human, and how to remain credible while the ground shifts underneath.

He does that work as a speaker, an advisor, and a builder. On stage, he translates complexity into language people can use. In an engagement, he maps decision authority and writes the governance that holds it. In production, he designs and deploys the agentic systems that the arguments are about, which is a useful corrective to advising from a distance.

His farm practice is not a metaphor. It is a working operation and a discipline for observing feedback loops, resource constraints, and long-horizon stewardship, and it is where technology claims get tested against weather, soil, labour, and seasons. It also anchors The Future Herd, the podcast and convening practice he hosts for the people navigating the future of food.

Media has run through all of it for three decades: hosting, interviewing, commentating, moderating, and explaining complicated systems to general audiences without flattening them.

## Working areas

- AI literacy, agentic deployment, and institutional governance
- Authority, legitimacy, and public presence after the platforms
- Disinformation and narrative conflict as operating conditions
- Food systems, open-source agrifood, and agricultural policy
