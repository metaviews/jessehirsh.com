If you are organizing a conference, retreat, summit, or strategic convening, you want more than a talk. You want a moment that clarifies what is happening, shifts how people think, and gives them language they use afterwards.

## What audiences leave with

- A framework for making decisions under uncertainty instead of reacting to headlines
- Clear language for AI, media systems, and institutional legitimacy without hype
- Better questions: the kind that reshape strategy, policy, and public communication
- A shared map of tensions and trade-offs, so teams stop talking past each other
- A sense of agency: what can be done now, by real people, in real institutions

## Formats

**Keynote (45 to 60 minutes).** A tight narrative arc, high clarity, strong Q&A.

**Fireside or interview (30 to 45 minutes plus Q&A).** For audiences that need signal over spectacle.

**Workshop (90 to 180 minutes).** Decision mapping, frameworks, and group sensemaking. Teams leave with something they can act on.

**Facilitation and moderation.** Panels, roundtables, and stakeholder conversations designed for emergence rather than theatre.

**Briefing.** A closed session for an executive team or board working through a specific decision.

## The four threads

Every engagement draws on one or more of these. Start with the argument closest to your problem.

- [Autonomy in automated systems](/new/autonomy/): AI literacy, delegation, and oversight that holds.
- [Authority after the platforms](/new/authority/): legitimacy when it no longer transfers on its own.
- [Food systems and the programmable farm](/new/food/): what agriculture reveals about what AI can and cannot do.
- [Building, not just advising](/new/building/): the deployment and governance practice behind the argument.
