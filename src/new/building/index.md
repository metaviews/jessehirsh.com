## The argument

Most people who talk about AI governance have never shipped a system. Advice that survives contact with an organization tends to come from someone who has, because the interesting problems are never in the architecture diagram. They are in the data nobody documented, the escalation path nobody tested, and the decision that turns out to have been made by a default setting.

So this is the part of the practice that builds. I design and deploy agentic systems, and I write the governance that goes around them. The two belong together: a framework written before the system exists describes an organization that does not exist either.

## What I build

**Agentic systems.** From scoping and architecture through to production deployment. Grounded in what agentic AI can actually do in an operational environment, not what it promises in a demo.

**Governance frameworks.** Acceptable use, oversight structures, accountability chains, escalation protocols, and alignment with the organization's stated ethics. Built from the inside out, tied to specific systems and specific risks, so that they hold when something goes wrong rather than only while everything works.

**Research and briefings.** Custom written work on a specific question: emerging risk, policy trajectory, technology assessment, competitive landscape. Scoped to the decision in front of you.

**Facilitation.** Multi-stakeholder convenings, retreats, and roundtables with conflicting interests and real stakes in the room.

**Workshops and training.** Team-level AI literacy and leadership cohorts, built around your context and your current level of exposure.

## Evidence, not slides

The clearest demonstration is a system in production. The Future Herd runs on an agentic production platform I designed: daily multi-source monitoring, an LLM-scored editorial queue, a knowledge graph of people and organizations, bilingual publishing, and distribution, all behind a dashboard with a human editor in the loop. It is the same architecture I would propose to you, with the failure modes already discovered the expensive way. [See the output](https://thefutureherd.ca).

## How engagements start

A conversation about the decision in front of you, then a scoped proposal with a defined deliverable. Retained work is available for leadership teams that need a steady outside view rather than a project with an end date.
